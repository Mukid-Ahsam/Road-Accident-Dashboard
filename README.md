# Road Accident Dashboard (Built with Microsoft Excel)

![Road-Accident-Dashboard](https://github.com/Mukid-Ahsam/Road-Accident-Dashboard/blob/main/RD%20Accident%20Dashboard.png)

## Project Object
The objective of this project is to analyze road accident data from 2021 and 2022 to uncover key patterns and factors contributing to traffic casualties. By visualizing the data in an interactive Excel dashboard, the goal is to help stakeholders—such as traffic authorities, safety analysts, and policy makers—identify high-risk conditions (e.g., specific road types, surfaces, or vehicle categories) and make informed, data-driven decisions to enhance road safety and reduce accidents.

## Questions (KPIs)
- What is the total number of casualties recorded across all years?
- What are the total casualties reported specifically for the years 2021 and 2022?
- How many casualties are categorized as Fatal, Serious, and Slight?
- What is the percentage distribution of casualties by severity (Fatal, Serious, Slight)?
- How do casualties compare between Rural and Urban areas?
- Which top 10 cities have reported the highest number of casualties?
- What is the breakdown of casualties by vehicle type involved?
- What is the monthly trend of casualties for the years 2021 and 2022?
- What is the distribution of casualties across the days of the week?
- How are casualties distributed based on speed limits at the time of the incident?
- What is the distribution of casualties by road surface condition?
- What is the breakdown of casualties by weather conditions during the incidents?
- What is the distribution of casualties by road type?
- Can a year-based filter be applied to dynamically view data for specific years?

## Process Of The Project
-	Verify the data for any missing values removed duplicates, standardized formats, and created calculated fields like attrition rate and age groups
-	Created PivotTables to summarize attrition across departments, job roles, age groups, education, gender, and marital status.
-	Used bar charts, pie charts, and tree maps to visualize key insights clearly and effectively.
-	Added slicers to allow users to filter by department and education level for dynamic data exploration.
-	Organized KPIs, charts, and visuals into a single dashboard sheet with consistent styling and layout for a professional look.

## Project Insights

- Total casualties from road accidents over two years are 271,404, with a drop from 144,553 in 2021 to 126,851 in 2022.
- 86% of the casualties were slight, 13% serious, and only 1% fatal, as per the severity pie chart.
- Most accidents occurred in urban areas (68%), compared to 32% in rural regions.
- Car occupants suffered the most with 217,052 casualties, far more than motorcyclists (21,996) and van drivers (21,372). And Bus-related casualties total 8,193, while agricultural vehicle accidents were significantly fewer at 644.
- The monthly trend shows 2022 consistently had fewer casualties than 2021 throughout the year.
- Weekday-wise, Friday saw the most casualties, followed by Tuesday and Wednesday, while Sunday had the fewest.
- The city with the highest number of casualties was Birmingham (5,839), followed by Manchester and Sheffield.
- Dry road conditions had the highest number of casualties (185,894), with wet/damp roads next at 73,773.
- Fine weather was reported during most accidents (223,599 cases), while only 35,285 occurred in rainy conditions.
- The most dangerous speed limit zone was 30 mph, with 184,330 casualties, drastically more than higher-speed zones.
- Single carriageways saw the majority of casualties (202,628), followed by dual carriageways (34,388).
- Roundabouts, one-way streets, and slip roads had significantly fewer casualties comparatively.
- The overall trend highlights that vehicle type, road condition, and urban location heavily influence accident rates.

## Conclution
The project presents a comprehensive dashboard analyzing road accident data from the years 2021 and 2022. It visualizes key insights such as total casualties, severity levels, vehicle types involved, urban vs rural distribution, and environmental factors like weather and road conditions. The dashboard also highlights monthly and weekday trends, city-wise breakdowns, and the impact of speed limits and road types on accidents. Designed for quick decision-making, it provides a clear overview to help identify patterns and improve road safety measures.
